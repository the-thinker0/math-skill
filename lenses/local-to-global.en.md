# 📈 Local-to-Global Lens

> From the particular to the general, from the known to the unknown — induction discovers patterns, analogy transfers experience, but neither constitutes proof.

## What Perspective It Offers

This is an "explorer's" perspective — diving vertically into finite specific cases to uncover general laws, or bridging horizontally to transfer the deep structure of one domain into another. It distinguishes the logic of discovery from the logic of verification: inductive reasoning generates conjectures, analogical transfer generates inspiration, but both require subsequent rigorous verification. Rigor is a tool of verification, not of discovery — Pólya.

## What Problems It Is Suited to Diagnose

- Observing a common pattern across multiple cases and formulating a general hypothesis
- Determining whether a property of a recursive structure (tree / list / expression) holds universally
- Cross-disciplinary borrowing — whether structures from other fields can be transferred to the current problem
- Refining hypotheses from counterexamples, iteratively converging toward more precise propositions

## What Problems It Is Not Suited For

- Situations requiring rigorous proof rather than conjecture — induction and analogy produce only hypotheses
- Cases with only one or very few samples — induction requires sufficient diversity and coverage
- Domains that are merely superficially similar — analogy demands structural isomorphism / homomorphism, not mere surface resemblance
- Problems that can be resolved directly by deduction — there is no need to take a detour through induction

## Which Knowledge Domains It Routes To

- Mathematical induction (weak / strong / structural / transfinite): rigorous proof tools for elevating conjectures to theorems
- Abstract algebra (homomorphisms / isomorphisms): formalization of analogy — precise measures of structural similarity
- Probability and statistics: the probabilistic version of inductive reasoning is statistical inference
- Algebraic geometry: the deep mechanisms underlying cross-domain structural transfer and compression

## What AI Designs It May Produce

- Iterative explorer: cases → patterns → hypotheses → counterexamples → refinement
- Analogy validity quantification: systematic mapping of components / relations / operations, outputting a structural similarity score
- Lakatosian hypothesis revision engine: monster-barring / lemma-incorporation / proof-strategy revision — select one of three

## Reasoning Protocol

1. **Collect cases**: Systematically gather concrete instances, covering boundary values, typical values, and extreme values to ensure diversity
2. **Identify patterns**: Compare commonalities and differences; remain alert to Borwein-type risks where "a pattern holds for many cases and then suddenly fails"
3. **Formulate hypotheses**: Prefer strong hypotheses; a hypothesis must be precise enough to be falsifiable
4. **Search for counterexamples**: Actively seek instances that falsify the hypothesis — this is the critical step that distinguishes science from pseudoscience
5. **Prove or revise**: Select the appropriate induction variant and attempt a proof; use Lakatosian methodology to revise the hypothesis in light of counterexamples, iterating until stable

## Acceptance Criteria

- The case list covers boundary values and extreme values, with sufficient quantity and diversity
- The hypothesis type is annotated (strong / weak / conditional) and is precise enough to be falsifiable
- A counterexample search has been executed; if counterexamples were found, the revision method is documented
- Analogies (if any) are supported by a quantitative structural similarity assessment, not merely intuition
- Inductive reasoning and inductive proof are clearly distinguished — the former produces conjectures, the latter verifies them
